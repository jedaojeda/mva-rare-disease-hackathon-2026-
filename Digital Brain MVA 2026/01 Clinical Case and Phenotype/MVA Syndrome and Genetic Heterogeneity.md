# 🔬 Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy (MVA) Syndrome

**Mosaic Variegated Aneuploidy (MVA)** is an ultra-rare autosomal recessive disorder characterized by constitutional chromosomal instability, severe pre- and postnatal growth failure, microcephaly, developmental delay, and a high lifetime risk of early childhood malignancies.

---

## 🧬 Genetic Classification and Heterogeneity (OMIM)

Three distinct genomic subtypes are recognized:

1. **MVA Type 1 (OMIM #257300)**:
   - **Causal Gene**: *BUB1B* (15q15.1), encoding the Spindle Assembly Checkpoint (SAC) kinase BUBR1.
   - **Mechanism**: Loss of checkpoint arrest, leading to premature chromatid separation (PCS).
   - **Oncologic Phenotype**: Highest incidence of Wilms tumor and embryonal rhabdomyosarcoma.
   - **Index Proband**: Resolved as MVA1 via biallelic compound heterozygosity ([[BUB1B Compound Heterozygous Causal Architecture]]).

2. **MVA Type 2 (OMIM #614114)**:
   - **Causal Gene**: *CEP57* (11q21), encoding a 57 kDa centrosomal protein required for microtubule nucleation.
   - **Mechanism**: Centrosome detachment and spindle pole fragmentation. Lower incidence of solid embryonal tumors.

3. **MVA Type 3 (OMIM #617598)**:
   - **Causal Gene**: *TRIP13* (5p15.33), an AAA+ family ATPase remodeling p31comet/MAD2.
   - **Mechanism**: Impaired MCC disassembly and delayed checkpoint silencing.
